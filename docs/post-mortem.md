## What's next for this project

The repo is yours. Decide what to do with it now that the course is over.

**Decide on visibility.** In your repo Settings → General → Danger Zone, you can switch between **Private** and **Public** at any time:

- **Public** — anyone can see your code. Good if you want this on your CV, LinkedIn, or as something to point recruiters to. Make sure there are no secrets in the history first.
- **Private** — only you (and people you invite) can see it. Good if it's a work-in-progress, contains data you don't want to share, or you're not ready to show it.

You can start private and switch to public later when you feel it's ready.

**Make it portfolio-ready.** Whatever visibility you pick:

- Rewrite the root `README.md` as a short project pitch: what it does, who it's for, screenshots, how to run it
- Write a short case study — the problem, your approach, what you learned
- Add it to your CV and LinkedIn with a link to the repo or a demo
- Keep iterating after the course — even small improvements show initiative

A project you built from scratch and can talk about confidently is one of the strongest things you can bring to a job interview.
